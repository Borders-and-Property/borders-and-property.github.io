About the project

Borders and Property is a three-year research project (2025–2028) funded by the Scientific and Technological Research Council of Türkiye (TÜBİTAK, Project No. 225K044).

The project investigates the transformation of property relations along the Ottoman–Greek border during the territorial negotiations and border changes of 1877–1883. Drawing on Ottoman, Greek, and British historical sources, it examines how shifting political boundaries affected property claims, legal practices, and relations between landholders and state authorities.

Bringing together historians and computer scientists, the project develops digital approaches to the analysis and integration of multilingual historical sources. Its research combines archival investigation, historical data extraction, spatial analysis, and computational methods.

Research

Historical context, sources, and methods

Historical context

Between 1877 and 1883, the Ottoman–Greek border underwent a series of diplomatic negotiations and territorial changes. The Treaty of Berlin (1878) and the Convention of Constantinople (1881) played central roles in redefining the frontier, particularly in Thessaly and Epirus.

These developments raised questions concerning the ownership, transfer, and legal status of landed property. The project investigates how property relations were negotiated and transformed in the context of changing political sovereignty.

Research questions

The project addresses three interconnected questions:

  • How did the reconfiguration of the Ottoman–Greek border affect existing property relations and claims?
  • How did Ottoman and Greek authorities, landholders, and other actors negotiate competing claims to property?
  • How can the integration of multilingual historical sources contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of border formation and property transformation?

Digital humanities methodology

The project develops an interdisciplinary research workflow organized around four interconnected processes.

  1. Document processing

    Digitization, image enhancement, transcription, optical character recognition (OCR), and preparation of multilingual historical texts.

  2. Historical data extraction

    Identification and classification of historical actors, places, properties, legal procedures, and events.

  3. Data integration

    Organization and cross-referencing of information from Ottoman, Greek, and British sources through structured datasets and historical identifiers.

  4. Historical analysis and visualization

    Spatial analysis, network analysis, and digital visualization of historical property relations and border transformations.

Throughout these processes, historians guide the interpretation and validation of computational outputs. Particular attention is given to source provenance, historical ambiguity, and the methodological challenges of integrating heterogeneous archival materials.

Digital resources

Datasets, tools, and visualizations

Overview

The project develops digital resources to support the investigation of historical property relations and territorial transformations. These resources are being created through an iterative process of archival research, data preparation, computational analysis, and historical validation.

Thessaly Border Gazetteer

  1. Planned
  2. In development
  3. Published

The Thessaly Border Gazetteer is a historical geographical dataset designed to identify and locate settlements and other geographical entities appearing in sources related to the Ottoman–Greek border.

The gazetteer brings together Ottoman, Greek, and modern place names, geographical coordinates, and supporting references. It documents the sources and degree of certainty associated with geographical identifications, enabling researchers to evaluate alternative place-name matches and historical ambiguities.

The gazetteer will support the spatial analysis of property relations and border changes.

Multilingual historical corpus

  1. Planned
  2. In development
  3. Published

The project is assembling and processing a multilingual corpus of Ottoman, Greek, and British historical documents concerning territorial negotiations, property claims, and administrative transformations.

The research workflow combines computational text-processing methods with historian-guided transcription, interpretation, and validation. Structured data extracted from these sources will support comparative historical analysis.

Historical maps and spatial data

  1. Planned
  2. In development
  3. Published

The project develops historical maps and spatial datasets to examine the geographical dimensions of border negotiations, territorial transfers, and property disputes.

By integrating historical cartography, archival references, and geographical data, these resources will facilitate the spatial investigation of changing political boundaries and property relations.
