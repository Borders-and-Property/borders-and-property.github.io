About the project

Borders and Property is a three-year research project (2025–2028) funded by the Scientific and Technological Research Council of Türkiye (TÜBİTAK, Project No. 225K044).

The project investigates the transformation of property relations along the Ottoman–Greek border during the territorial negotiations and border changes of 1877–1883. Drawing on Ottoman, Greek, and British historical sources, it examines how shifting political boundaries affected property claims, legal practices, and relations between landholders and state authorities.

Bringing together historians and computer scientists, the project develops digital approaches to the analysis and integration of multilingual historical sources. Its research combines archival investigation, historical data extraction, spatial analysis, and computational methods.

Research

Historical context, sources, and methods

Historical context

Between 1877 and 1883, the Ottoman–Greek border underwent a series of diplomatic negotiations and territorial changes. The Treaty of Berlin (1878) and the Convention of Constantinople (1881) played central roles in redefining the frontier, particularly in Thessaly and Epirus.

These developments raised questions concerning the ownership, transfer, and legal status of landed property. The project investigates how property relations were negotiated and transformed in the context of changing political sovereignty.

Research questions

The project addresses three interconnected questions:

  • How did the reconfiguration of the Ottoman–Greek border affect existing property relations and claims?
  • How did Ottoman and Greek authorities, landholders, and other actors negotiate competing claims to property?
  • How can the integration of multilingual historical sources contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of border formation and property transformation?

Digital humanities methodology

The project develops an interdisciplinary research workflow organized around four interconnected processes.

  1. Document processing

    Digitization, image enhancement, transcription, optical character recognition (OCR), and preparation of multilingual historical texts.

  2. Historical data extraction

    Identification and classification of historical actors, places, properties, legal procedures, and events.

  3. Data integration

    Organization and cross-referencing of information from Ottoman, Greek, and British sources through structured datasets and historical identifiers.

  4. Historical analysis and visualization

    Spatial analysis, network analysis, and digital visualization of historical property relations and border transformations.

Throughout these processes, historians guide the interpretation and validation of computational outputs. Particular attention is given to source provenance, historical ambiguity, and the methodological challenges of integrating heterogeneous archival materials.

Digital resources

Datasets, tools, and visualizations

Overview

The project develops digital resources to support the investigation of historical property relations and territorial transformations. These resources are being created through an iterative process of archival research, data preparation, computational analysis, and historical validation.

Thessaly Border Gazetteer

  1. Planned
  2. In development
  3. Published

The Thessaly Border Gazetteer is a historical geographical dataset designed to identify and locate settlements and other geographical entities appearing in sources related to the Ottoman–Greek border.

The gazetteer brings together Ottoman, Greek, and modern place names, geographical coordinates, and supporting references. It documents the sources and degree of certainty associated with geographical identifications, enabling researchers to evaluate alternative place-name matches and historical ambiguities.

The gazetteer will support the spatial analysis of property relations and border changes.

Multilingual historical corpus

The project is assembling and processing a multilingual corpus of Ottoman, Greek, and British historical documents concerning territorial negotiations, property claims, and administrative transformations.

The research workflow combines computational text-processing methods with historian-guided transcription, interpretation, and validation. Structured data extracted from these sources will support comparative historical analysis.

Historical maps and spatial data

  1. Planned

The project develops historical maps and spatial datasets to examine the geographical dimensions of border negotiations, territorial transfers, and property disputes.

By integrating historical cartography, archival references, and geographical data, these resources will facilitate the spatial investigation of changing political boundaries and property relations.

Team

An interdisciplinary research collaboration

Fatma Öncel

Principal Investigator. Bahçeşehir University, Istanbul.

Dr. Fatma Öncel is a historian specializing in Ottoman social and economic history, with a focus on rural history, land and property relations, and the Balkans. She is a faculty member at Bahçeşehir University’s Center for Ottoman Studies (BAU-OTAM) and a research affiliate at Stanford University’s Center for Spatial and Textual Analysis (CESTA). She also serves as a trustee of the Tarih Vakfı (History Foundation) and on the editorial boards of the peer-reviewed journals Turkish Historical Review and Toplumsal Tarih Akademi. Her research examines how tax, labor, and property institutions shaped political and economic structures, alongside the methodological possibilities of digital approaches to historical archives. She received her Ph.D. in History from Boğaziçi University in 2018, with a dissertation on agrarian relations and estate agriculture in Ottoman Thessaly. She is a recipient of Brill’s Middle East and Islamic Studies Early-Career Paper Prize, and her research has appeared in journals including the Journal of the Economic and Social History of the Orient, Turkish Historical Review, International Review of Social History, and Middle Eastern Studies.

Dr. Öncel is the Principal Investigator of the Borders and Property project, where she leads the research agenda and coordinates an interdisciplinary team of historians and computer scientists. Through this project, she investigates the transformation of property relations along the Ottoman–Greek border between 1877 and 1883, combining multilingual archival research with digital humanities methods to examine the intersections of territorial change, political sovereignty, and property regimes.

Berna Kamay Ulusay

Researcher. Sabancı University, Istanbul.

Berna Kamay Ulusay is a legal historian of the late Ottoman Empire. She holds a Ph.D. in History from Boğaziçi University (2022), with a dissertation titled Extradition in the Ottoman International Legal Practice of the Nineteenth Century. She received her M.A. in History from Bilkent University and her B.A. in American Culture and Literature from Hacettepe University. Her research explores international law, the politics of legal belonging, and the diplo-legal encounters through which jurisdiction and nationality were negotiated across trans-imperial worlds. She holds a teaching position at Sabancı University’s Foundations Development Programme, where she offers courses on the late Ottoman Empire and World History.

Within the Borders and Property team, Berna Kamay Ulusay investigates French diplomatic and legal sources preserved in the Ottoman archives. Her work focuses on how questions of property transfer and nationality were raised, contested, and settled.

Ayşe Ozil

Researcher. Sabancı University, Istanbul.

Ayşe Ozil is an Associate Professor of History in the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences at Sabancı University. Prior to joining the university, she held a postdoctoral position at Princeton University and a fellowship at Leiden University. She received her PhD from the University of London, Birkbeck College. Her research explores the social history of the late Ottoman Empire and early Republican Turkey, focusing on social networks and institutions, urbanization, and property. She is the author of Orthodox Christians in the Late Ottoman Empire: A Study of Communal Relations in Anatolia (Routledge, 2013) and various articles in journals such as Turkish Historical Review, Middle Eastern Studies, and Journal of the Ottoman and Turkish Studies Association. She served as a senior researcher on the ERC-funded Ottolegal and the UC Berkeley-based Istanpolis projects, and her work has been supported by TÜBİTAK, Koç University’s ANAMED, and the American School of Classical Studies. Currently, she has two primary research interests: the first explores communal property ownership practices, and the second examines the intersection of global trade networks, property patterns, and urban development through commercial buildings (hans), both in late Ottoman Istanbul. This latter research resulted in a digital exhibition co-curated with Sadettin Fidan: The “Han”s of Galata (2022).

Within the Borders and Property team, Ozil investigates the relationship between the management of rural property and Galata bankers, and contributes to the digitization project through a comparative analysis of the categorization of concepts derived from Ottoman and Greek source material.

Firuzan Melike Sümertaş

Researcher.

Firuzan Melike Sümertaş is an urban and architectural historian whose research focuses on the urban, architectural, and visual culture of the late Ottoman Empire and its capital, Istanbul, with particular attention to the Greek Orthodox communities. She holds a PhD in History from Boğaziçi University, along with MA and B.Arch degrees from METU. Her doctoral dissertation traced the transformation of antiquarian interest in Byzantine Constantinople into urban archaeological research over the long nineteenth century. Dr. Sümertaş was awarded the Fulbright Cultural Exchange Scholarship at Princeton University and a TÜBİTAK Postdoctoral Research Fellowship at the University of California, Berkeley, where she also held a teaching position. She has additionally held scholarships from ARIT-Istanbul, AKMED-Antalya, the École française d’Athènes, BTU-Cottbus, and the Gennadius Library-Athens. Besides the Borders and Property team, she is currently the co-curator of the exhibition “All Phanar is Here: Household, Neighbourhood, Court and the City,” currently open at the ANAMED (Koç University, Istanbul) Gallery, and co-editor of the accompanying book with the same title. Dr. Sümertaş is also involved with the IstanΠόλις (www.istanpolis.org) collaborative, where she conducts research on the urban and architectural history of nineteenth-century Greek communities in Istanbul, with a particular focus on utilizing digital humanities tools for historical research.

Within the Borders and Property team, Dr. Sümertaş oversees research on Greek-language newspapers and archival sources, including their transcription, analysis, and datafication. She also coordinates the transcription and analysis of Ottoman archival materials, as well as the datafication and visualization of the resulting information.
